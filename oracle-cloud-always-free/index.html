What You Actually Get (Always Free Tier)

Oracle's "Always Free" tier is not a 30-day trial. It is a permanent, no-expiration resource allocation. Here is the exact quota as of May 2026:

The headline number is the ARM allocation: 4 cores and 24 GB of RAM. That is not a toy server. That is a machine that can comfortably run a Node.js gateway, multiple isolated agent workspaces, a small vector database, and still have headroom for experiments.

On the storage side, 200 GB of boot volume is enough for multiple environments, datasets, and model caches without constant cleanup. And 10 TB of monthly egress means you can serve content, stream logs, or run public APIs without ever thinking about bandwidth charges.

Why This Matters for AI Agent Work

Most AI agent frameworks need a persistent process somewhere. OpenClaw, CrewAI, LangGraph — all of them need a machine that stays online to receive cron triggers, process heartbeats, and hand off work between agents.

The typical starting point is a $4–6/month VPS from DigitalOcean, Vultr, or Linode. That gets you 1 CPU core and 512 MB to 1 GB of RAM. It works, but it is tight. You feel the constraint every time you add a new agent or install a dependency.

Oracle Cloud's free ARM server gives you 4× the cores and 24× the RAM of a basic $5 droplet, at zero cost. The difference is not just comfort — it is capability. With 24 GB, you can:

  • Run an OpenClaw Gateway with 5+ isolated agents
  • Host a local vector store (Chroma, Qdrant) for retrieval workflows
  • Keep multiple model inference containers warm
  • Run background scraping jobs without stealing resources from active agents
  • Experiment with heavier models on CPU (Llama 3.2, Mistral 7B) without swapping to disk

It is the difference between a system that "technically works" and a system that has room to grow.

The Signup Process (2026 Updated)

Oracle Cloud registration has a reputation for being finicky. Cards get declined, regions sell out, accounts get flagged. But as of early 2026, the process is straightforward if you follow a few specific rules.

Here is what works, based on field testing and the 零度解说 (Lingdu) tutorial that has been tracking Oracle's registration flow for years:

What You Need

  • A real credit or debit card with Visa or MasterCard support. Virtual cards and prepaid cards often fail. Oracle charges a small pre-authorization (~$1) that is refunded within days.
  • A residential internet connection. Do not use a VPN during signup. Oracle checks IP reputation, and commercial VPN IPs are heavily flagged.
  • A phone number that can receive SMS. The field accepts most formats.

What You Can Keep Flexible

Contrary to popular belief, strict identity matching is not required:

  • Name: Can be entered in pinyin or English. Does not need to match the card exactly.
  • Address: Can differ from the billing address. A general city-level address is sufficient.
  • Phone: Does not need to be the number tied to your bank.

The only thing Oracle actually validates is the card itself. If the pre-auth succeeds, the account is approved.

Region Selection

During signup, you choose a "home region." This cannot be changed later without opening a new account. Choose based on latency:

If you plan to run infrastructure for a Chinese-speaking audience, Tokyo or Seoul are the obvious choices. If you are building for a global audience, US West gives you the most headroom.

Creating the Instance

After account activation:

  1. Navigate to Compute → Instances → Create Instance
  2. Under Image and Shape, choose Oracle Linux 9 or Ubuntu 22.04 (both are free-tier eligible)
  3. For Shape, select VM.Standard.A1.Flex (ARM)
  4. Set OCPUs: 4, Memory: 24 GB
  5. Add a boot volume of 200 GB
  6. Generate and download the SSH key pair
  7. Create

The instance will not display an "Always Free" badge in the console, but as long as your total ARM allocation stays within 4 OCPU / 24 GB, you will not be billed. Oracle's billing system tracks this automatically.

The honest assessment: Oracle Cloud free tier is not a production platform. There is no SLA, support is community-only, and high-demand regions occasionally run out of ARM capacity. If your site generates revenue or serves customers, a paid VPS is still the safer choice.

But if your use case is "run AI agents, learn multi-agent orchestration, host personal projects, or build a proof-of-concept pipeline," the free tier is genuinely better than most paid entry-level plans. The Real Caveats

Before you rush to sign up, four warnings:

1. Account dormancy policy. Oracle can reclaim idle resources. Log into the console at least once every few months and keep the instance running light tasks. A cron job that touches a log file daily is enough to show activity.

2. No Always Free badge on ARM. The console will not reassure you with a "free" label on ARM instances. You must manually confirm your allocation stays under 4C/24G. Check the tenancy "Limits" page for peace of mind.

3. One account per person. Oracle actively deduplicates accounts. Multiple signups from the same IP, card, or identity will trigger suspension. If you need another region, the legitimate path is opening a second tenancy under a different email and card — not gaming the same account.

4. Egress is generous but not unlimited. 10 TB is more than enough for 99% of personal projects, but if you are running a video streaming site or large-file CDN, you will eventually hit the ceiling.
