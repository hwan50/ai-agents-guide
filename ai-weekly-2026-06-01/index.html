The Inference Pivot

What AI's Direction Change Means for Capital

Anthropic just passed OpenAI in valuation on one-third the revenue. NVIDIA is buying its own customers. Broadcom is about to announce something on June 3. The industry is not slowing down — it is changing direction.

Argument 01

The Valuation Inversion

I want to start with the fact pattern, because the fact pattern is the entire argument.

The New York Times reported this week that Anthropic has overtaken OpenAI as the most valuable artificial intelligence company in the world. Anthropic's 2024 revenue was roughly $1 billion. OpenAI's was roughly $3.7 billion. And yet the market has priced Anthropic higher.

In any framework that values companies by revenue, this is incoherent. The smaller producer should trade at a discount, not a premium. The market's verdict, then, is that we are not in a framework that values AI companies by revenue.

The market is pricing something else. It is pricing the premium that enterprise customers will pay for AI they consider trustworthy — a 20% to 40% markup over commodity inference, by the pricing logic of the compliance-cloud market. OpenAI may ship faster. GPT-5.5 may iterate ahead. Braintrust and Codex may be the better code-generation workflow. But the market has already voted, and it voted for safety.

The valuation inversion has a name, and it isn't scale.

Argument 02

The Inference Pivot

What we are in, instead, is a framework where AI's economic value is migrating from training to inference. This is not a side trend. It is the dominant structural shift — and it deserves a name. Call it the Inference Pivot.

The Inference Pivot rests on three supports, each independently real, and each independently priced.

The first is demand. Goldman Sachs research estimates AI Agents will push token consumption up 24×. That is not a marginal increase. It is a demand-structure inversion that moves inference from 30% of total compute spend to 70%. The businesses that are already AI companies — Microsoft, Amazon — will absorb the first wave. The businesses that are not will absorb it later. But the direction is uniform.

The second is cost. Pinterest stripped the vision layer from its model and cut AI costs by 90%. MeMo memory models improve performance 26% without retraining. The marginal return on model-slimming and inference optimization is rising faster than the marginal return on scale training. That changes the economics of deployment, not just the economics of development.

The third is architecture. Broadcom does not make general-purpose GPUs. It designs custom ASICs — TPU for Google, AI silicon for Apple. In inference, a custom ASIC delivers token costs an order of magnitude below a general-purpose GPU. When inference dominates the compute stack, that gap stops being a marginal advantage and becomes structural tilt. The June 3 announcement that multiple outlets are predicting will be the first test of whether that tilt is already priced in or still arriving.

NVIDIA sees the same vector. $3.8 billion — the amount it spent this week acquiring two AI application companies. Against a $3 trillion market cap, the dollars are negligible. But the direction is the entire signal. NVIDIA is not buying chip competitors. It is buying its customers — the companies that run on NVIDIA hardware. The shovel-maker is becoming a shareholder in the gold mines. CUDA's moat is deepening, but its form is shifting from technical lock-in to capital lock-in.

I do not think the Inference Pivot thesis is wrong. I think it is early, and the market is already pricing its endpoint.

Argument 03

The Two-Speed Market

Intel's stock is up nearly 500% in the past year. But this is a low-base rebound — it traded near $20 in 2025. Gaudi 3 holds less than 5% share in LLM inference. The 500% is narrative, not product.

The contrast is ITBench-AA: frontier models score below 50% on enterprise IT tasks. In coding and customer service, agents are operationally viable. In permission management and ticket routing, they are not.

Goldman Sachs predicts 24× token demand growth. ITBench proves agents cannot handle basic enterprise work. Both are true.

The contradiction is an artifact of conflating two markets. The 24× is coming from the top 5% of enterprises — the ones that are already AI companies — and from validated verticals like coding and customer service. The sub-50% scores describe general-purpose agents in broader enterprise contexts. Both realities coexist, but they are separated by an 18-to-24-month maturity gap.

The implication is temporal, not directional. Inference chips rise first. Agent platforms rise later. Timing the gap matters more than calling the direction.

Argument 04

The Policy Split

Two stories landed on the same day. Reuters reported new U.S. export restrictions on NVIDIA chips to China. Oracle announced a contract to deploy AI on classified clouds for the Department of Defense.

The coincidence is not coincidence. The global AI market is being split in two by policy.

On one side is the government-compliance market: high unit prices, long contracts, strict certification, reliability over cost. Oracle's deal validates the hypothesis that governments will pay a premium for secure AI. That market is valued at $30 billion to $50 billion, growing over 25% annually, and pricing at 1.5 to 2× the consumer tier.

On the other side is the consumer market: transparent pricing, brutal competition, rapid iteration. NVIDIA's China-related revenue is roughly 20% to 25% of total. The export restrictions cut that stream off. The government contracts are replacing it.

OpenAI and Anthropic are not yet in the government market — neither has FedRAMP certification. The premium is going to systems integrators and cloud providers. The AI labs built the models. The policy framework is deciding who gets paid for them.

The Redistribution

America is restricting NVIDIA's access to 20-25% of its revenue base. It is simultaneously creating a government AI market valued at $30-50B with 1.5-2× pricing power. The net effect is not a loss. It is a transfer — from consumer-margin chips to government-margin contracts, from NVIDIA's P&L to Oracle's.

Argument 05

The Physical Ceiling

There is one variable most analyses skip because it is uncomfortable: electricity.

AI power consumption is expanding at 300% annually. A large training run consumes roughly 10 MWh. H100 and B200 cluster deployments are already constrained by data center power capacity. Google and Microsoft are siting new facilities next to nuclear plants.

Energy efficiency — inference per watt — is replacing peak FLOPS as the competitive dimension for chips. And data center construction timelines have stretched from 12 months to 24 months. That physical constraint may bind the industry before chip supply does.

Apple is moving Siri and core AI functions to on-device processing in iOS 27. Google demonstrated Gemini running on-device at I/O. Edge AI is shifting from experimental to scaled, potentially moving 20% to 30% of inference compute off the cloud within a year. Apple Silicon and Qualcomm benefit not because they are faster than data center GPUs, but because they are faster than the power grid.

The trade is not to pick winners in each layer. The trade is to notice that the market has already priced the endpoint of every thesis — Anthropic's Trust Premium, Broadcom's ASIC tilt, NVIDIA's capital lock-in, the Policy Split's redistribution — while the physical infrastructure to support them is still being built. The market is pricing certainty about direction without pricing uncertainty about timing.

Three events in the next 48 hours will test which of these theses are priced in and which are still arriving.

Broadcom's June 3 announcement. If it confirms large-scale inference ASIC or data center orders, the structural-tilt thesis passes its first stress test.

Apple's iOS 27 AI details. Whether Siri runs on an Apple model or a Google Gemini backend determines whether edge AI is a platform shift or a distribution deal.

Oracle's DoD contract follow-through. If Amazon and Microsoft announce similar classified-cloud deployments, the government-premium thesis is confirmed as a market-wide repricing, not a single-company anomaly.

The market has already chosen. Your job is to notice which bets are fully priced and which are still building.

The Trust Premium is a real thesis. The Inference Pivot is a real thesis. The Policy Split is a real thesis. They are also, in different corners of the market, fully priced ones. Those are the same sentence.
